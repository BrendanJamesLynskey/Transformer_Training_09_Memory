01

Sixteen Bytes per Parameter

LLaMA 7B’s weights take 13.5 GB at two bytes each, yet training it on one device needs far more. With mixed-precision Adam (decks 04 and 08) every parameter costs:

BF16 weight 2
+
BF16 gradient 2
+
Adam m 4 + v 4
+
FP32 master 4
=
16 bytes

That is ZeRO’s accounting (Rajbhandari et al. 2020, §3.1, \(K = 12\) bytes of optimiser state including the master copy). Plain FP32 Adam also costs 16 (4 + 4 + 8). For LLaMA 7B (6,738,415,616 parameters by the calculator; Touvron et al. 2023, Table 2, lists 6.7B) that is 107.8 GB before a single activation.

02

Activations: What the Backward Pass Needs

The backward pass needs, at every operation, what the forward pass computed there (deck 03). Korthikanti et al. (2022) count it for a GPT layer with 16-bit activations:

$$\text{bytes per layer} = s\,b\,h\,\Big(34 + \frac{5\,a\,s}{h}\Big)$$

for sequence length \(s\), micro-batch \(b\), width \(h\) and \(a\) heads. The \(5as/h\) term is attention’s \(s \times s\) score and weight matrices, one per head (Part 1, chapter 3): it grows with the square of the sequence. For LLaMA 7B at sequence 4,096 and one sequence per step that is 104.2 GB over 32 layers, about as much again as everything else, and it doubles with every doubling of the micro-batch. The whole budget per device:

$$M = 16N + L\,s\,b\,h\,(34 + 5as/h) + 6\,s\,b\,V$$

Even with full recomputation, the 16 bytes per parameter remain and the peak is still over 100 GB: more than any single accelerator holds. Memory, not compute, is what first forces a model to be split across devices.

05

Activation Checkpointing: Full and Selective

Full recomputation

Keep only each layer’s input (\(2\,sbh\) bytes) and, during the backward pass, recompute the layer’s forward pass to get the rest back (Chen et al. 2016). It costs one extra forward pass per step: roughly a third more compute, since backward costs about twice forward.

Selective recomputation

Korthikanti et al. keep everything but the attention scores and weights (the \(5as/h\) term), which are cheap to recompute and expensive to store: \(34\,sbh\) per layer. Most of the activation saving, recomputing only the cheap part.

For LLaMA 7B the peak falls from 199.5 GB with no recomputation to 116.1 GB (selective) and 110.7 GB (full). With recomputation, the backward pass re-creates one layer’s full activations at a time on top of the checkpoints, which the calculator counts.

06

Counted on Part 1’s Model

The formula can be checked against a real backward pass. Part 1’s model keeps 2,320 values per sequence per block for its backward pass, counted in the reference trainer’s forward pass; Korthikanti et al.’s formula for the same shape gives 2,496 (results.md §10).

Why they differ in detail

The formula assumes a feed-forward layer four times the model’s width (Part 1’s is twice) and dropout masks (Part 1 has none).

Why they agree in size

Both count the same things: inputs to every linear layer, GELU’s pre-activation, attention’s weights, each norm’s input. It is the activations, not the weights, that grow with the sequence and the batch.

For Part 1’s whole model in mixed precision: 5,376 parameters × 16 bytes = 86,016 bytes of state, and a peak of 286,208 bytes per step at 16 sequences of 8: about a quarter of a megabyte.

07

Still Too Much for One Device

The next step is to stop every device holding everything:

Shard the state: ZeRO / FSDP

Split optimiser states, gradients and finally weights across the data-parallel workers: 16 bytes per parameter divided by the number of workers (deck 10).

Split the model

Tensor parallelism splits each layer; pipeline parallelism splits the stack; sequence parallelism splits the activations (deck 11).

The site’s notebook 10 measures peak memory (torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated) with and without checkpointing a 12-layer model on a GPU: GPU run pending; the calculator is not yet checked against it.

08

What to Take Away

  • Mixed-precision Adam: 16 bytes per parameter (2 + 2 + 8 + 4) before any activation. LLaMA 7B: 107.8 GB.
  • Activations: \(sbh(34 + 5as/h)\) per layer, growing with the sequence squared and with the batch.
  • Checkpointing trades about a third more compute (full) or a little (selective) for most of the activation memory.
  • Past one device’s memory, shard the state (ZeRO) or split the model.
